Now the katha of the Bhil woman. There was a Bhil woman living in the forest. Picking fruit, small fruits such as pilu, or piyar, chironji, she filled a basket and brought them to sell. O you who want fruit, buy the fruits. Take fruit, buy fruit. When she was passing Nanda Baba's house and said, buy fruit, the moment he heard it Achyuta ran quickly, to take fruit. He went first. He went with grain in the hand. Before this Krishna had seen that Mother Yashoda, when she bought some fruit, went with grain from the house. Even today this thing is in the village. Someone comes to the village to sell something, and the woman goes with grain. He went with grain in the hand. Wheat, or rice, whatever it was, he picked it up quickly and ran. He gives every fruit to everyone. Dharma, artha, kama, moksha, heaven, liberation, everything is in his hand. When the Lord is worshiped by sacrifices, the Lord gives heaven. Learned people ask for heaven, and I give them heaven. Those who want Vaikuntha, who want my abode, I give the abode. And in this world also, those who want wealth, fame, their own people, he gives them everything. But that same Lord, who is the giver of every fruit, today became one who asks for fruit, and to pay the price of the fruit he picked up grain in the joined palms and went. In walking he was a child. He was walking, stepping and stumbling. He does not even know how to close the palms properly. So by the time he reached that Bhil woman, all the grain in the hand had fallen. Wherever he put a foot, he was spilling still more, shaking. This too is an uncommon lila. All the grain fell. The Lord who could not even keep the grain safe in his own hand. Vishvanatha Chakravarti writes that two or four grains were stuck, only in the fingers. Both hands had become empty of grain.

Then that Bhil woman filled the child's hands with fruits, pilu or chironji and the rest, taking them from her basket. She wanted to give more, but only that much could be held. The poor woman did not wait for the grain to arrive and then give. The fruit seller, his two hands from which the grain had fallen, she filled with fruits, and the fruit basket was filled with jewels. [Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.11.11] The child was so beautiful, so attractive, so bright, that a feeling of vatsalya like Mother Yashoda's came into her mind. She had brought very beautiful fruit. She knew that Nanda Baba has one boy, and it is to be taken and given to him. It is the family of the king. If she had said, I will give it later, they would have felt shy. So she was saying, buy the fruit. She filled the Lord's empty hands with fruit. Where she gave fruit into the Lord's hands, her basket filled with jewels. With gold, with diamonds.
