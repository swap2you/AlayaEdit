# Note for Auni — Vatsalya Lila, part 9

English first, then Hindi.

This is part 9. About 92 minutes. HH Radha Govind Das Goswami Maharaj. It continues the sentence where part 8 stopped, and it ends after the calves and the boys have gone into Aghasura's mouth. He has not swallowed them. Krishna has not yet gone in.

A visitor to Vrindavan should plan the gram and the fruit for the elders, the monkeys, the pigeons, and the peacocks. The boys make faces with the monkeys, leap with the frogs, and jump the small rivers. They laugh at their shadow in the water, and they scold their own echo. The speech is heroic, not abuse. A person is made unhappy by his own voice.

Then the verse Prabhupada loved. Saints taste Krishna as the joy of Brahman, and only in meditation. Servants taste him as their Lord, and even in Vaikuntha they do not play like this. To Braj, and to the bewildered, he is a human child. It is the same child. Closing the eyes is not yet seeing. The gopis ask how long meditation can console them. The highest joy is playing with him.

Sridama wins the game. The winner rides, the loser is the horse. A king's son is nothing in a game. Krishna sulks, weeps, and becomes the horse. Nine lakh cows do not make the boys unequal. At Dvaraka the doorkeeper asks which Brahma. Four faces, then eight, then thousands. Among those elephants, Sanaka's father is a rabbit. Krishna asks whether the creation is going well.

The cause is heaps of piety. Feed the hungry. Rantideva, Bali, and Dadhichi are named. The greatest piety is reminding a person of Krishna, because forgetfulness is the sorrow. The name purifies what austerity, sacrifice, and charity do not. The chanter may forget. The Lord does not. Shukadeva says the assembly can play too, if the heaps are made. One dust of his feet the yogis do not obtain, and he is standing in front of the eyes of Vraja. A monkey there is greater than Brahma of another place. Shukadeva says he cannot describe that fortune.

Then a different beginning. They had forgotten breakfast in the play. A great demon named Agha, and agha means sin. A saint helps without being taught. A wicked person harms in the same easy way. The gods wanted him dead because he disturbed their worship. He was sin by birth, then Kamsa's company, and a salary for it. Putana and Baka are the sister and the brother. Balarama is not with them today. He will kill the boy with the whole party, and the parents will die like a tree whose root is pulled. They never look at what the sister and the brother had done. Surpanakha weeps before Ravana. The jester asks why she went to Panchavati, and Ravana does not ask. Maricha warns him and still goes, choosing to die by Rama's hand.

On the path he lies as a python, one yojana in every direction, thick as a mountain. The tongue is a road. The breath is a forest fire. The boys have not wandered everywhere, so they take him for a new beauty of Vrindavan. They agree that it looks like a python, and they still call it stone: cloud, cliff, peaks, path, darkness, and the hot wind. If it swallows them, the enemy of Baka will finish it, as he finished the crane. They look at Krishna's face, clap, and run. The calves go first. He lives in every heart, so he knows it is a demon. While he is deciding to stop them, they are inside. The python does not close the mouth or twist the body, because Krishna would escape. They have entered. They have not been swallowed. The sitting stops there.

Hindi in the captions uses standard spelling. The verses are from the book, with the citation in brackets. The horse, the many Brahmas, and the Ramayana are given as Maharaj told them.
