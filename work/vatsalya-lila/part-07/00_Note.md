# Note for Auni — Vatsalya Lila, part 7

English first, then Hindi.

This is part 7. About 91 minutes. HH Radha Govind Das Goswami Maharaj. It continues the sentence where part 6 stopped, and it ends as Bakasura is introduced.

Upananda is still speaking. Somehow the child was saved from Putana. By Hari's mercy the cart did not fall on him. Love itself makes them forget that Krishna overturned it. Trinavarta took him into the sky and fell on a stone. The cowherds had not touched him with a stone. Kalidasa is cited for the heart that fears a misfortune for someone it loves. Six or seven days earlier the two trees fell, the child did not die, and no other child died. They knew the child had thrown the trees.

He proposes they leave before the next calamity, with the children, the priests, the servants, and the cows. First the place must be fixed. Vrindavan has grass and water, and it stays new. It is the whole circle of Braj, not four kos. They will cross the Yamuna by boat. The cows will swim. Upananda says, today. Yoke the bulls. Send the cows ahead, because they graze as they go. Everyone says excellent, excellent, with one intelligence. Sanatana: the intelligence is in Krishna alone. No one says but. Prabhupada is cited against putting if on a thing that is known.

The young men walk with bows. Elders, women, and children ride. The gopis sing the childhood lilas from the bullock carts. This is not a horse chariot. Akrura brings the first chariot they will have seen. Yashoda and Rohini sit on one cart, in the middle, with Krishna and Balarama. The boys argue in a lisp about a tiger.

They enter Vrindavan and set the carts in a half moon. The mouth of the dwelling faces one way. A husband who cannot protect is called a tea leaf. Rama took only bow and arrow and brought Sita back. Peace is said to come from power. Shivaji is named. Folded hands did not establish it.

Seeing Vrindavan, Govardhana, and the banks of the Yamuna, Rama and Madhava feel a happiness they had not had even in Vaikuntha. The lisp gives a conscious joy, not a worldly one. They become keepers of calves, not yet keepers of cows. Prabhupada: they were not sent to school, because a vaishya learns the cow. Jiva Goswami: about two and a half years, and Maharaj reads it as two to three and a half. They play near the village. The flute is not yet extraordinary. They skip stones, sound the ankle bells, and fight as pretend bulls. The seven bulls of Nagnajit are told as the training this play is for.

On the Yamuna a demon sent by Kamsa enters the calves. Krishna shows him to Balarama as if bewildered, catches the hind legs and the tail, whirls him dead, and throws him onto a kapittha tree. The boys say excellent. The gods rain flowers. No weapon. The Gita is quoted: the evildoers and the bewildered do not surrender. The maintainers of all the worlds have become keepers of calves. On other mornings they eat, then herd. Only the morning of Aghasura they leave before food. At a lake east of Nandagaon, not named as the Yamuna, the calves drink first. Then a white crane, bright as goodness, stands in meditation on the fish. He is Putana's brother. Aghasura is also her brother. They fell into Kamsa's company. The sitting stops there.

Hindi in the captions uses standard spelling. The verses are from the book, with the citation in brackets. Kalidasa and the Nagnajit story are given as Maharaj told them, without a verse number he did not speak.
